*AI didn't just change software. It broke the hardware market we built our hobby on.*

If you've priced out a new gaming PC recently, you already feel it. RAM that used to be an afterthought in your budget is now one of the most expensive line items in your build. GPUs are harder to find and more expensive than they should be. Prebuilts that would've been solid deals a year ago are either stripped down or shockingly overpriced. And everywhere you look, the answer is the same: AI did this.

Welcome to RAMageddon. And I'm here to tell you it's not going away anytime soon.

---

## The Numbers Hit Different When It's Your Build

A 32GB DDR5 kit that sold for $100–$200 in October 2025 now starts at $350 — if it's even in stock. And DDR4 isn't the safe haven it used to be either: the same kits that cost $60–$90 just months ago are now running $150–$180. *(Tom's Hardware)*

The sweet spot used to be a 32GB DDR5 kit at 6000MHz — it saw the steepest percentage price hike precisely because it was the most popular configuration. The part that every budget-conscious PC builder gravitated toward is now the one that got hit hardest. *(The CyberSec Guru)*

And if you're trying to dodge the DDR5 tax by sticking to an older platform? The shift to DDR5 is now mandatory for new AMD AM5 and Intel builds. You cannot save money by sticking to DDR4 on new high-end boards. You're paying the new prices whether you like it or not. *(The CyberSec Guru)*

Top analyst firm Gartner predicts the sub-$500 entry-level PC segment will disappear by 2028, along with a worldwide PC shipment decline of 10.4% in 2026. For a hobby that's always had a price-of-entry problem, that is a genuinely alarming trajectory. *(PC Gamer)*

---

## Your GPU Problem Is Also a RAM Problem

Most PC gamers know about the DRAM crunch in system memory. Fewer realize the GPU market is being squeezed by the exact same forces.

GDDR6 and GDDR7 — the memory on your graphics card — come from the same manufacturers under the same supply pressure. The rapid growth of AI data centers is straining supply chains for both DDR5 and graphics memory like GDDR6 and GDDR7, and AMD has already warned partners that memory pricing has risen enough that its next wave of GPUs will be priced higher. Nvidia is rumored to be planning similar increases. *(NoobFeed)*

And then there's this: Nvidia is reducing gaming graphics card production by up to 40% in 2026 and has indefinitely delayed the RTX 50 Super series to prioritize AI accelerators. For the first time in nearly three decades, Nvidia is actively deprioritizing its gaming division. *(PressPlay Finance)*

Read that again. Nvidia — the company that built its brand on GeForce — is cutting gaming GPU production nearly in half to feed AI demand. NVIDIA accounts for roughly 90% of SK Hynix's HBM supply. The GPUs that were supposed to be your next upgrade are sitting on the same fab wafers that Nvidia would rather sell to a data center. *(Introl)*

---

## This Shortage Is Structurally Different

PC gamers have lived through hardware shortages before. The COVID chip crunch. The crypto mining GPU boom. Both were painful. Both eventually ended.

This RAM shortage is categorically different. It's not a disruption — it's a structural shift. AI data centers aren't going anywhere. *(PressPlay Finance)*

Here's the core problem: HBM — the high-bandwidth memory that AI accelerators run on — requires a 3-to-1 trade ratio with DDR5, meaning every HBM wafer displaces three DDR5 wafers. Every single AI chip being manufactured right now is directly suppressing the supply of the memory in your PC. The two markets aren't separate — they're competing for the same finite fab capacity. *(OpenMetal)*

Suppliers are reporting record gross margins of 60–70% for HBM — much higher than for standard DRAM. So manufacturers have every financial incentive to keep making the premium stuff for data centers and let consumer supply stay tight. This isn't a bug in the system. From their perspective, it's a feature. *(Manufacturing Dive)*

---

## New Fabs Won't Save Your 2026 Build

"Just wait for supply to catch up" is the conventional wisdom. The problem is the timeline.

Micron's new Idaho fab doesn't begin production until 2027. SK Hynix's Yongin facility completes around the same time. Getting back to normal pricing and availability is more like 2028–2029 — and only if AI infrastructure spending slows down. *(OpenMetal)*

Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan has bluntly stated there will likely be "no relief until 2028," and that's the base case — if AI demand accelerates further, the horizon could push beyond 2028. *(Tech Insider)*

Micron has already killed its Crucial consumer memory brand in favor of AI data centers. The company that made the RAM in millions of gaming builds decided the consumer market wasn't worth maintaining a storefront for. That's how lopsided the economics have become. *(tomsguide)*

---

## The Practical Gaming Playbook Right Now

So what do you actually do? Here's the honest take:

**Don't wait for prices to drop this year.** The "hold off and buy the dip" strategy is exactly backwards in this market. RAM prices now behave like stock market quotes — the same modules can jump significantly over just a few weeks. Waiting is not a strategy right now; it's just paying more later. *(ICP DAS)*

**If you're on AM4 or an older Intel platform, stay there.** If you're on a solid DDR4 platform like AM4, stick with it. The performance jump to DDR5 for gaming is real, but at current prices the FPS-per-dollar value proposition is terrible. Wait for the 2027 correction if you can. *(The CyberSec Guru)*

**Buy more RAM now, not less.** If you're building or upgrading, don't cheap out on memory to save money today. A 16GB system bought at peak prices might need an expensive upgrade in two years when RAM is even pricier. Go to 32GB once and be done with it.

**Prebuilts have actually gotten more competitive.** It is now cheaper to buy a prebuilt gaming PC than building one yourself — and the RAM price crisis is only going to make that gap wider. If that stings your builder pride, I get it. But it's the math right now. *(tomsguide)*

**Temper your GPU upgrade expectations.** With Nvidia cutting gaming production and GDDR7 constrained, don't expect the next-gen GPU refresh to be well-stocked or reasonably priced at launch. Plan around what's available, not what's announced.

---

## The Bottom Line

The gaming hardware market as we knew it — cheap RAM, frequent affordable GPU refreshes, accessible entry-level builds — was built on the assumption that memory would keep getting cheaper over time. AI broke that assumption, possibly permanently.

PC gaming has always had a gatekeeping aspect to the price of entry. Now it's becoming downright ridiculous. *(PC Gamer)*

The fabs being built today will eventually help. The margins being earned today will eventually attract enough investment to rebalance things. But "eventually" is 2028 at the optimistic end, and that's assuming the AI buildout doesn't keep accelerating.

In the meantime: be strategic, extend what you have, and don't let FOMO push you into a bad buy. The hobby isn't going anywhere — it's just going to cost more to stay in it for a while.
